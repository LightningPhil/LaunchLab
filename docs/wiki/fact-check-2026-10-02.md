# Wiki science and history audit

Reviewed: 2 October 2026.

## Scope and method

The review covers all 18 articles at all three reading levels (54 readings), all five advanced nozzle topics at all three levels (15 more readings), article statistics and equations, moon-family membership, the solar and belt maps, diagram captions, exhibit histories, tooltips, readouts and model explanations. The cannon, rocket and nozzle calculations were reviewed alongside the prose so that an explanation agrees with the experiment it describes.

Facts were checked against agency data, the IAU's adopted resolution, museum collections and original research. Links were opened during the review, rather than relying solely on their titles. Values are intentionally rounded for learning. This audit checks the wiki's claims and teaching models; it does not validate them as predictions of flight hardware. The game's invented worlds and physics settings are outside this content audit.

## History and model evidence

- **Early cannon:** [Tonio Andrade's research chapter](https://www.tonioandrade.com/_files/ugd/88be10_6d1a981ca79946488ba646c3630d550a.pdf), printed pp. 253–254, discusses small surviving Chinese guns dated 1288 and 1298. The [Royal Armouries](https://royalarmouries.org/objects-and-stories/stories/the-hundred-years-war-1337-1453), Artillery section, supports the 1326 Florence record and vase-shaped gun/bolt illustration. Neither proves an exact invention date.
- **Shot materials:** [Royal Museums Greenwich's stone-shot record](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-37115) and [UCL's Mary Rose research poster](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/bartlett/sites/bartlett/files/characterising_cast_iron_cannonballs.pdf) substantiate stone, iron, lead and composite shot. The exhibit's equal-size uniform balls are a simplification, not a reconstruction of one recovered load.
- **Space gun:** [NASA's archived HARP account](https://pwg.gsfc.nasa.gov/stargaze/Smartlet.htm) supports 1960s gun-launched atmospheric instruments and extreme acceleration. A high suborbital trajectory is distinct from attaining orbit.
- **Rocket milestones:** [NASA's rocket history](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/TRC/Rockets/history_of_rockets.html), [Goddard biography](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/robert-goddard/) and [space-age timeline](https://www.nasa.gov/history/dawn-of-the-space-age/) support the medieval origins, 16 March 1926 and 4 October 1957 dates used by the exhibit. Unrelated stories in the archived history are not imported into the wiki.
- **Cannon mechanics:** pressure difference times area, net force over mass and net work changing kinetic energy agree with [Newtonian mechanics](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/newtons-laws-of-motion/). The model's gas-energy balance includes heat release, expansion work and losses. Its charge, material masses, gamma and time units are stated classroom choices.
- **Travelling rocket:** the fixed effective exhaust speed, varying mass and ideal logarithmic velocity change agree with the [ideal rocket equation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/ideal-rocket-equation/). Burn rate changes duration and thrust; identical mass ratios give identical ideal final velocity changes. Gravity, drag and real chemistry are explicitly omitted. Sound and star drift are explanatory cues.
- **Advanced nozzle:** the area–Mach and isentropic pressure relations agree with [NASA nozzle physics](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/nozzle-design/). The implementation obeys `ṁ = pc At / c*`, `F = ṁ ve + (pe − pa) Ae` and `Isp = F / (ṁ g₀)`, with SI conversions for bar and millimetres. The three fixed gamma/c* pairs and broad mixture curves are illustrative, not measured propellant-performance tables or chemical-equilibrium solutions.
- **Auto and separation:** sea-level Auto matches exit/ambient pressure within the model range. Vacuum Auto selects the largest allowed area ratio because nozzle mass is omitted. Mixture Auto maximises the explicitly invented curve. [NASA separation research](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19780022611) shows that correlations depend on nozzle/flow conditions; the lab's 0.4-ambient clipping rule cannot predict real shock structures or side loads.
- **Historical engine pressures:** [NASA *Remembering the Giants*, Appendix C, printed p. 106 / PDF p. 107](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/sp-4545.pdf#page=107), visually inspected because the table is scanned, gives the F-1 reference as **1,125 psia**, or **77.57 bar**. The [NASA engine study, printed p. 21 / PDF p. 29](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19780003243/downloads/19780003243.pdf#page=29) gives SSME **20.7 MPa / 3,000 psia**, or approximately **207 bar**. These support the rounded historical labels, not every engine version's operating pressure.

## Source interpretation and limitations

Official educational pages sometimes simplify terminology or use different rounding. The Venus overview's sunrise-to-sunset wording is not used to overwrite the correct 117-day **solar-day** explanation; NASA's dedicated climate account and technical literature distinguish it from the 243-day sidereal rotation. ESA directly supports the wiki's 464 °C / 92-pressure comparison. JPL's satellite table explicitly identifies mean radius, avoiding an unlabelled precision comparison.

All retained original source URLs were retrievable in the browsing review; the exception was the removed Raptor post. The added Uranus dynamo PDF required a direct download because browser extraction failed. Its PDF page 34 was then rendered and visually inspected, confirming that the thin-shell dynamo is presented as a model. These access results are a dated observation, not a permanent link-availability guarantee. Oceans inferred below ice, capture scenarios and interior dynamo hypotheses remain expressed at the appropriate level of uncertainty.

New supporting references are linked in the relevant articles:

- [NASA solar interior](https://solarscience.msfc.nasa.gov/interior.shtml) and [OpenStax fusion feedback](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/22-4-nuclear-fission-and-fusion) support energy transport and the Sun’s stabilising response.
- [NASA seasons](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/seasons/en/) and [Earth’s energy budget](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/climate-and-earths-energy-budget/) support the illumination and energy-flow explanations. No dated energy-budget percentages were imported.
- [Gale Crater evidence](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/a-guide-to-gale-crater/) and [MAVEN atmospheric loss](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasas-maven-reveals-most-of-mars-atmosphere-was-lost-to-space/) support the expanded Mars readings. Present escape rates are not extrapolated unchanged through planetary history.
- [NASA barycentres](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/barycenter/en/) and [Hubble’s Ganymede observations](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasas-hubble-observations-suggest-underground-ocean-on-jupiters-largest-moon/) support shared motion and the auroral evidence for a conducting ocean. An ocean model remains distinct from a uniquely established interior or evidence of life.
- [Agnor and Hamilton’s capture research](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature04792) supports the proposed binary route for Triton; it is explicitly described as a modelled scenario. [NASA’s ice-giant energy review](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasa-oxford-discover-warmer-uranus-than-once-thought/) supports Neptune’s internal energy contribution. Older claims that Uranus has no internal heat were not adopted.
- [NASA’s Vesta account](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/asteroids/4-vesta/) supports early melting and differentiation. The asteroid reading does not assume all metal-rich objects have one uniquely known origin.

Worked examples were checked against the stated equations: 4 AU gives an approximately eight-year solar orbit; doubling a circular orbital radius gives approximately 2.8 times the period; Neptune’s solar illumination scales approximately as 1/30²; doubling throat diameter quadruples area; O/F = 3 gives oxidiser/fuel mass fractions of 3/4 and 1/4; ideal rocket mass ratios of two and four give ln(2) and ln(4) velocity factors. The nozzle descriptions were also checked against the actual fixed-γ/c* implementation, including the cancellation between mass flow and exhaust velocity when only c* changes.
